The alignment matrix

Step through the fill, have it ask you what goes in the next cell, or fill the whole grid and have it marked. It opens on the two protein fragments from the lecture, scored by plain identity; switch Scoring to BLOSUM62, then Alignment to local, and watch the answer change. Change the sequences and the gap cost too, or press New pair for a fresh example.

Three modes sit above the matrix. Step through advances one cell at a time and says where each value came from; the Fill all button beside it completes the rest in one go, for when you want the traceback and not the arithmetic. Guess a cell picks cells scattered over the matrix and asks you for each in turn. Fill it in hands you the empty grid and marks what you type, which is the one worth doing before the exam. The sequences are editable, so a pair of your own works as well as ours, and changing the gap cost with a matrix on screen shows which alignment survives the change and which was an artefact of the penalty. Nothing you type leaves the tab: this is a static file, and there is no server to send a sequence to.

The gap cost here is linear, charged per gap position; real programs use an affine penalty that charges more to open a gap than to extend one, which Session 4 explains and this page cannot show. See also how BLAST works and profile HMMs.
