How BLAST works

Dynamic programming compares two sequences by filling a whole matrix. A database search cannot: a million sequences is a million matrices. BLAST fills almost none of them. Step through the six things it does instead, and watch the counter at the bottom, which is the entire argument. Then lower the threshold and see what the fast setting was missing.

The defaults are the real ones, taken from the programs rather than from memory: blastp prints Neighboring words threshold: 11 and Window for multiple hits: 40 in the footer of every report, and blastn prints neither, because a nucleotide search has no neighbourhood and extends from a single hit. The protein database is six real globins from the Pfam seed alignment used in Session 4; the nucleotide one is synthetic, so that the identities are exact and the odds can be worked out in advance. The one simplification is the gap cost, which is linear here and affine in the real programs. See also the alignment matrix.
